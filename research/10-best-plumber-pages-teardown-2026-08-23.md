### 14. The German bait cluster and the Verbraucherzentrale (what NOT to do)
Search on the commercial German terms returns "Abflussreinigung ab 39 €", "Festpreis ab 29 €" and "Festpreise ab 49 €" alongside serious operators at 149. Those anchors are the German twins of the Belgian 20 euro and 52 euro baits in `research/07`.
The Verbraucherzentrale names four practices: night surcharges applied to the whole bill instead of only to labour, invoicing work that did not clear the blockage, billing for specialist equipment that was never used, and double billing by the hour AND by the metre of pipe. It quotes a 1,100 euro toilet unblocking where the blockage remained.
Verbraucherzentrale Niedersachsen adds the call centre trap: free 0800 numbers that look like a local firm and route to one national call centre, with a referral commission hidden inside the customer's bill. Their advice to consumers is exactly our page brief: ask for the call-out cost, the hourly rate and a binding total price, and walk away from anyone who refuses figures on the phone.
It also says a fixed price set on the phone with pressure to pay cash is itself a red flag. So a fixed price alone is not enough: it must come with a written trace, a real address and a real company number.
**Use:** this is the source for our "how to avoid getting ripped off" block, and the reason the page must show the enterprise number, the address and a written confirmation, not just a number said on the phone.

### The CRO numbers worth quoting (Web Tonic, 47 plumber sites studied)
Optimised landing pages convert at 5 to 15 percent against 1.5 to 3 percent for a home page, top performers 12 to 16 percent. 75 to 85 percent of conversions are phone calls, and phone leads close at 46 percent against 2 to 5 percent for form submissions.
Gaps found across the 47 sites: 40 percent had no analytics, 35 percent no trust signals, 30 percent no clear CTA above the fold, 25 percent no prominent phone number.
The sticky click to call header is called "the highest conversion impact available on any plumbing landing page". Removing one form field lifts form conversions 5 to 10 percent. Moving Google reviews above the fold lifts trust based conversions 8 to 15 percent. CTA colour and placement tests give 10 to 25 percent. A headline matching the ad gives 15 to 30 percent Quality Score improvement. Average mobile load is 8.6 seconds against a 2.0 second target.

## (a) The 15 patterns the strongest pages share

1. **One promise, stated as a number.** 5 dollars a minute (Benjamin Franklin), 149 euro Festpreis, 169 euro vast tarief, 99 euro TTC. Adjectives are free, numbers are expensive, so numbers are believed.
2. **Price certainty beats speed.** Every market converges: "prix annoncé = prix facturé", "Der genannte Preis ist der Endpreis", "you'll never pay more if the repair takes longer", "Vaste prijs vooraf". 24/7 is table stakes.
3. **The surcharge is answered before it is asked.** "No hidden or extra charges on holidays, nights, and weekends", "geen weekend-, avond- of spoedtoeslag", "sans majoration soir & week-end".
4. **The call-out fee has a stated boundary.** Free, included, or a named figure with a radius. Never silence.
5. **The phone number IS the button text.** "Bel direct 085 401 51 97", "Appelez le 01 86 98 33 88", "Call 24/7 365 days 0808 253 8514".
6. **A sticky element that never leaves the screen.** Top bar (Drain Doctor, 247Ontstopper), fixed header (Zoom Drain), bottom call button (Arizona).
7. **A four icon promise bar directly under the hero.** Dyno-Rod: 24/7, fixed price, no call-out, 3 month guarantee. Drain Doctor and 247Ontstopper use the same shape.
8. **A four step "what happens next" block.** Contact, free call-out, quote, work begins. It turns fear of the unknown into a sequence.
9. **A guarantee with a duration.** 30 days (Aprime, Arizona), 3 months (Dyno-Rod, DrainExpress), 6 months (247Ontstopper). Never "satisfaction guaranteed" on its own.
10. **Short form or no form.** Two fields (Dyno-Rod, 247Ontstopper), four (Drain Doctor). The long forms belong to the weakest pages in the set.
11. **Named humans.** Engineer names inside testimonials, a technician name and photo texted before arrival, first name plus initial under reviews.
12. **Real job photography, organised by job type.** Aprime's gallery, 247Ontstopper's eight work photos, Drain Doctor's engineers. Nobody strong uses generic stock.
13. **The service area named exhaustively, plus a catch-all line.** Postcode field, then the list of towns, then "if your town is not listed, call us anyway".
14. **Legal identity in the open.** Real street address, company or VAT number, and a note that the call is not premium rate ("Appel non surtaxé").
15. **The customer's own words as headings.** "Verstopt?", "Blocked Drain?", "Küchenspüle verstopft?", "Combien coûte vraiment un débouchage ?"

## (b) The 10 mistakes the weak ones make

1. Reviews loaded by a third party script that is still empty when the page renders (Roto-Rooter showed "Loading customer reviews..." on fetch).
2. A seven field callback form with two consent checkboxes on an emergency page (Mr. Rooter).
3. Asking for an e-mail address from someone whose cellar is flooding (Pimlico, Mr. Rooter, Arizona).
4. Discount banners for kitchens and bathrooms placed above the emergency services (Pimlico).
5. Hiding the phone number in the footer and pushing WhatsApp first (DrainExpress).
6. AI generated team photos on a trust page (Arizona shipped a file named ai-dalle-generated-cleaners-duo).
7. A 20 plus section page used as an Ads landing page (Aprime): good for SEO, slow and unfocused for a paid click.
8. Bait anchors, 29 or 39 euro in Germany, which force the upsell on arrival and produce the complaints the whole category suffers from.
9. Vague price language with no figure anywhere: "transparent pricing system", "up front estimates", "affordable".
10. Invented counters and unsourced satisfaction percentages, which is the local Belgian norm (`research/07`) and which our rules forbid anyway.

## (d) Proof that looks trustworthy with zero reviews

We have no reviews and we may not invent any. Placeholder cards marked for replacement belong in the design file, never on a live page. The strong pages show that reviews are only one of six proof types, and the other five are all available to us on day one.

1. **The identity block beats a star widget for a first time visitor.** Real street address in Vilvoorde, the enterprise and VAT number, a real e-mail, and a photo of the van with its plate visible. Mister Rohrreinigung and Aprime both lead with a street address. Belgian law requires it anyway (`research/03` 1.8), so we may as well make it a trust asset instead of footer text.
2. **The honest absence, stated once.** FR suggestion: "Nous démarrons notre page Google. Nous ne pouvons pas encore vous montrer d'avis, alors nous vous montrons notre travail." NL: "Onze Google-pagina start net. Wij hebben nog geen beoordelingen, dus tonen wij ons werk." This converts the weakness into the honesty claim the whole page is built on.
3. **Dated job cards instead of testimonials** (from Aprime's case studies). Town, month, the job, three photos, the price actually charged. Ten of those are worth more than ten anonymous five star quotes, and every one is verifiable.
4. **The equipment block as competence proof** (`research/03` S6). Photograph the camera, the jetting machine, the pump and the auger, with one line each on what they do. It answers the cowboy fear the way a badge would.
5. **The written trace as a promise.** A quote by SMS or WhatsApp before departure, an itemised invoice after. The Verbraucherzentrale treats the absence of a detailed invoice as the fraud marker, so publishing that we always give one is a real differentiator.
6. **A visible review ask, before there are reviews.** Mister Rohrreinigung shows "Jetzt auf Google bewerten!" next to its ratings. Ours reads FR: "Après l'intervention, nous vous demanderons un avis honnête, bon ou mauvais." NL: "Na de interventie vragen wij u een eerlijke beoordeling, goed of slecht." It signals that reviews are coming and that we do not filter them.
7. **When the first three real reviews exist**, add the Google widget but keep the job cards. Do not use a script that renders empty like Roto-Rooter's, and never put a spinner where proof should be.

## (e) Before and after imagery in this trade

1. **The pair is the format.** Aprime labels them "AVANT" and "APRÈS" inside one card, same angle, same framing. FR "AVANT / APRÈS", NL "VOOR / NA". Never two photos from two different jobs in one pair.
2. **The camera makes the pair credible.** A still from the camera inspection before, and the same length of pipe after, is proof that a phone photo of a clean toilet is not. Roro already owns the camera, so this is the cheapest proof asset in the business, and it doubles as the argument for selling camera inspection.
3. **Organise the gallery by job, not by date.** WC, évier, douche, sterput, égout, cave, fosse septique. A visitor who arrived on the "WC bouché" ad wants to see a WC. That is message match applied to photographs.
4. **Caption with facts, not adjectives.** Town, month, what was blocking it, how long it took. FR suggestion: "Grimbergen, juillet. Racines dans l'égout. Caméra puis haute pression, 1 h 15." NL: "Grimbergen, juli. Wortels in de riool. Camera en daarna hogedruk, 1 u 15."
5. **Never identify the customer.** No name, no house number, no street sign, no face, no address in the caption or in the frame.
6. **Keep it clean, not gross.** The strong pages show equipment, technicians and clear pipe. Extreme filth photos read as shock content and they sit badly next to a price table.
7. **Real, current, and ours.** No stock, no AI generated technicians (Arizona's own file name gives it away), no supplier photos. Hook Agency's summary of the category is that trucks and team beat stock, and that the page is winning a five second decision from a stressed person on a phone.
8. **Weight and speed.** These are photographs on an emergency page: WebP, capped width, lazy loaded below the fold, and never a carousel that autoplays. The category average mobile load is 8.6 seconds and the target is 2.
9. **A short video is the upgrade, not the day one asset.** A 15 second camera clip with no sound can be added later. It must never autoplay with sound and never block the first paint.
