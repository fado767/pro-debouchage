### 1.5 Ranking, hero verdict and before/after

**The five best photos for the page, in order**

1. **`lifestyle-12-Cropped.jpeg`** the whole promise in one frame: branded van at the kerb, technician already working, an ordinary Belgian house.
2. **`pro-debouchage-car-1.jpeg`** the best technical file we own, and the only one where the phone number, the name and the 24/7 badge are all sharp and readable.
3. **`lifestyle-10.jpeg`** the clearest "we unblock your toilet" picture, with the brand and the number on the technician's back in the same frame, bright and clean.
4. **`lifestyle-1.jpeg`** the equipment proof: a real Rioned UrbanJet built into the van, which separates this business from a man with a plunger.
5. **`lifestyle-11.jpeg`** the camera on a tablet with a distance counter, the detail customers do not expect and that justifies an inspection fee.

**Hero verdict: yes, `lifestyle-12-Cropped.jpeg`, with two conditions.**

I agree with the suspicion. On an emergency page the hero has one job, to say "a real local company will come to my house now", and this is the only frame that says all of it at once. Two conditions before it can be the hero:

- **It must be upscaled.** 770 px wide will not fill a desktop hero. A 2x upscale to about 1540x1926 is needed, and the neighbour's number plate must be re-checked after the upscale, because upscalers invent legible characters where there were none.
- **The customer's consent** should be asked for, since the house is identifiable to a neighbour.

If either condition fails, the fallback hero is `pro-debouchage-car-1.jpeg` in 16:9: weaker story, but sharper, wider, no people and no privacy question at all. A good compromise for the build: `car-1` as the wide desktop hero, `lifestyle-12-Cropped` as the mobile hero, since mobile is where the emergency calls come from.

**Before and after pairs that already exist: none.** There is no true pair in the set. The nearest thing is `temporary-low-quality-images/6.jpeg`, where the clean stripe and the dirty paving sit in the same frame. `lifestyle-4` (hole open) and `lifestyle-5` / `-6` (working in the hole) are a sequence of one job, not a before and after, and should not be labelled as one.

**Before and after generated pairs: no.** This is the clearest call in the report. A before and after is a claim about a specific job at a specific house. Generating one is a fabricated result, and a caption does not fix it, because the format itself is the claim and captions are not read. It would break rule 1 head on and it is exactly the kind of thing a competitor screenshots.

The alternative, in order of speed: **R1** upscale the real half-cleaned driveway (G5) and caption it honestly, "la même allée, en cours de nettoyage" / "dezelfde oprit, tijdens de reiniging". **R2** brief Afram in one WhatsApp line to shoot, on the next three jobs, one photo before he starts and one from the same spot when he finishes, phone held at the same height. Two real pairs beat ten generated ones and cost nothing. **R3** until a real pair exists, the page shows the camera monitor photos (`lifestyle-7`, `-11`) as the evidence, which is honest and specific.

### (c) Blunt: what is worth credits and what is vanity

**Worth it.** G1, G2, G3, G4, G5. Notice what they have in common: not one of them invents anything. They are a vectoriser, a background remover, a relight and two upscales applied to files Roro actually sent. That is where the money is, because they make the real assets usable at the size a modern page needs, and they carry zero rule 1 risk.

**Vanity.** G8, G9 and every video except possibly V1. A visitor with a blocked toilet at eleven at night decides in about eight seconds. A hero video costs LCP, costs credits, and risks a wrap with garbled letters on the one page where credibility is the product. Generated illustrations of services we cannot photograph are worse: they signal "stock website" to exactly the visitor we want, and they invite the rule 1 question we should never have to answer.

**The real gap is not generation, it is a shoot.** We have no septic emptying photo, no flooded cellar photo, no readable number plate, no daylight van photo at the depot, no before and after pair, and no portrait of Afram we are allowed to publish. One hour with a phone on the next visit fixes all six, for nothing. Every generated image on this list is a patch over that hour.

**Recommended final shortlist**

- **Images, six:** G1 (logo vector and transparent PNG), G2 (studio van 16:9), G3 (studio van 1:1), G4 (hero upscale of `lifestyle-12-Cropped`), G5 (upscale of the real half-cleaned driveway), G6 (one flat diagram of how a blockage forms).
- **Videos, two, and neither above the fold:** V1 only if the wrap text survives inspection, V3 as the second. If V1 fails inspection, ship the page with zero video and lose nothing.

### (d) Post-processing plan

**Order of operations.** Consent and plate check first, then upscale, then background removal, then crop to ratio, then export. Never export before the plate check, because a bad file is hard to recall once it is in a CDN.

**Export matrix.** Widths **480 / 800 / 1200 / 1600**, each in **AVIF** and **WebP**, with a JPEG at 1200 as the last fallback. `srcset` on every image, `sizes` set per slot, `loading="lazy"` and `decoding="async"` on everything except the hero, `fetchpriority="high"` on the hero only.

| Slot | Widths | AVIF target | WebP target | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hero (`lifestyle-12-Cropped` upscaled, or `car-1`) | 800 / 1200 / 1600 | under 120 KB at 1600 | under 180 KB at 1600 | Quality about 50 AVIF, 75 WebP. The hero is the LCP element, budget it first. |
| Service cards | 480 / 800 | under 35 KB at 800 | under 55 KB at 800 | Quality about 45 AVIF, 72 WebP. |
| Proof gallery tiles | 480 / 800 | under 30 KB at 800 | under 50 KB at 800 | Same. |
| Studio van on white (G2, G3) | 800 / 1200 / 1600 | under 90 KB at 1600 | under 140 KB at 1600 | Flat white compresses well. Also export a transparent PNG master. |
| Logo (G1) | SVG | under 12 KB | n/a | Plus a 512 px transparent PNG and a 32 px ICO for the favicon. |
| OG image | 1200x630 only | n/a | n/a | JPEG under 200 KB. Social scrapers are unreliable with AVIF. |
