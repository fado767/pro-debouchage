### 1.2 What the CRO literature says (numbers, with sources)

- 75 to 85 percent of home service conversions happen by phone, not by form. https://www.webtonic.io/blog/plumbing-landing-page-statistics
- A sticky bottom tap to call bar lifts mobile call volume by roughly 25 to 40 percent on its own, and needs a real tel: href, at least 48px height, high contrast, and nothing
overlapping it. Same source.
- Astra Results puts the button bigger still, 64 to 80px tall on mobile, and measures 1 to 1.5 points of conversion lift within 7 days.
https://astraresults.com/blog/post/may-2026/plumbing-website-conversion
- Above the fold on mobile (first 800px) must carry the H1 that matches the ad, the tap to call button, three trust signals, and one real photo. Same source.
- Load time: industry average on mobile is 8.6 seconds, the target is about 2 seconds, and every second past 3 seconds costs 7 to 12 percent of conversions. Both sources above.
- Dedicated service landing pages convert at 5 to 15 percent against 1.5 to 3 percent for a generic home page. https://www.webtonic.io/blog/plumbing-landing-page-statistics
- Keep any form to three fields (name, phone, short description). Each extra field costs 10 to 15 percent. https://www.apexure.com/landing-page-examples/plumbing-repair-landing-page
- "The phone number IS the CTA. When someone's kitchen is flooded, no form on earth will convert them." (quoted from the Apexure teardown, same URL).

### 1.3 Fifteen headline formulas, and the fear each one kills

| # | Formula | Pain point it answers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Problem as a question plus instant answer ("Blocked Drain? We'll be there today") | urgency |
| 2 | Service plus city plus 24/7 | availability at night |
| 3 | Price certainty in the headline ("prix annoncé avant de commencer") | the surprise bill |
| 4 | No surcharge promise (night, weekend, holiday) | being punished for calling at 2am |
| 5 | Free callout, déplacement gratuit | risk of paying for nothing |
| 6 | Named base town ("déboucheur basé à Vilvoorde") | local, not a call centre |
| 7 | Arrival window ("sur place aujourd'hui", "binnen X min") | speed |
| 8 | Equipment named (camera, high pressure, pump) | cowboy fear, will he even fix it |
| 9 | Pay after the job is done | fear of prepayment scams |
| 10 | Insurance report offered | damage fear, who pays for the water |
| 11 | Guarantee with a duration | fear it blocks again next week |
| 12 | Diagnosis before demolition (camera first) | fear of a destroyed floor or wall |
| 13 | One human on the phone, not a call centre | being passed around |
| 14 | Specific job named (WC, évier, cave, fosse septique, sterput) | message match with the ad |
| 15 | Legal identity in the open (BCE, VAT, address) | fear of an untraceable operator |

Note on formula 1 versus formula 3: Roto-Rooter, Mr. Rooter, Dyno-Rod, Drain Doctor and the whole German category lead on price certainty, not on speed. Speed is table stakes because
everyone claims 24/7. Price certainty is the real differentiator.

### 1.6 Recommended page structure, one page, mobile first

The call button is always in the viewport (sticky bar at the bottom on mobile, sticky header button on desktop). WhatsApp is a secondary, smaller button next to it, never the same size.

**S0. Sticky call bar (always visible).** Full width, 64px tall, high contrast, real tel: href, text "Appeler 0480 649 649" / "Bel 0480 649 649", plus a small WhatsApp icon button at 25
percent of the width. Nothing (no chat widget, no cookie banner) may sit on top of it once consent is handled.

**S1. Hero (first 800px on mobile).** Job: make the visitor call in under 8 seconds. Contains the H1 (matched to the ad group), one subheadline, the big call button, and a three item
trust strip. One real photo of the van or Roro at work behind or beside it. No form here.

**S2. Trust strip (inside or right under the hero).** Three items only, all honest on day one: "24h/24, 7j/7, week-end et jours fériés", "Basé à Vilvoorde, Bruxelles et Brabant
flamand", "Prix annoncé avant de commencer". Once the BCE number and insurance are confirmed, swap the third for "Entreprise enregistrée, BCE 0xxx.xxx.xxx".

**S3. The six services, as tappable cards.** Débouchage urgent, WC et évier, curage haute pression, inspection caméra, vidange fosse septique, pompage cave inondée. Each card is a short
paragraph plus its own call link. Job: message match with whichever ad group sent the visitor.

**S4. How it works, four steps.** Copied in shape from Drain Doctor and Ontstoppingsdienst Pro. 1. Vous appelez, on vous pose trois questions. 2. On vous annonce un prix. 3. On vient
avec le matériel. 4. On confirme le prix sur place et on débouche. Job: kill the fear of the unknown.

**S5. Prices, honestly.** A table with a starting price per job type, clearly marked "à partir de" / "vanaf", excl. or incl. VAT stated, plus the deplacement rule and the sentence "Le
prix est annoncé avant de commencer. Pas de majoration la nuit ni le week-end" (only if Roro confirms there is no night surcharge). If he does apply one, say so plainly with the amount.
Job: the single biggest conversion lever in this category.

**S6. What we bring (equipment).** Camera, high pressure jetting, pump, tanker access for septic. One photo each if possible. Job: cowboy fear, and it is the honest substitute for
reviews.

**S7. Zone d'intervention.** Vilvoorde first, then the named communes: the 19 Brussels communes, plus Machelen, Grimbergen, Zaventem, Steenokkerzeel, Meise, Wemmel, Kampenhout, Zemst,
Diegem, Strombeek, Peutie, Koningslo. Job: local relevance and a Quality Score assist. Do not claim all of Belgium.

**S8. Reviews section, day one version.** Do not fake, do not fill with stock testimonials. Show the Google review widget as soon as the Google Business Profile has at least three real
reviews, and until then run one honest line: "Nous démarrons notre page Google. Après l'intervention, nous vous demanderons un avis honnête, bon ou mauvais." Job: turn the absence of
reviews into a proof of honesty. This matches the agency rule on never faking anything.

**S9. FAQ, eight questions.** Themes taken from Dyno-Rod plus the local price fear: 1. Combien ça coûte ? 2. Y a-t-il un supplément la nuit ou le week-end ? 3. Le déplacement est-il
payant ? 4. En combien de temps êtes-vous là ? 5. Faut-il casser quelque chose ? 6. Faites-vous un rapport pour l'assurance ? 7. Quelles communes couvrez-vous ? 8. Comment puis-je payer
? Mark up as FAQPage JSON-LD.

**S10. Final call block.** Big button again, the phone number written out, the hours, the address.

**S11. Footer, legal.** Company name, address, BCE number, VAT number, e-mail, phone, links to privacy policy and cookie policy. See 1.8.

### 1.7 Mobile CRO details

- **Tap to call.** Real `<a href="tel:+32480649649">`. Also write the number as visible text so it can be copied on desktop. Never render the number as an image.
- **Button size.** 64 to 80px tall on mobile, full width minus 16px gutters, contrast ratio at least 4.5:1, thumb reachable at the bottom of the screen.
- **Load speed.** Target under 2 seconds on 4G. One hero image, WebP, width capped, lazy load everything below the fold, no autoplay video, no third party script except the consented
tag. Every second past 3 seconds costs 7 to 12 percent of conversions (source in 1.2).
- **No form above the fold.** A form may appear at S9 or S10 as a "rappelez-moi" option, three fields maximum (name, phone, problem).
- **Language switch FR / NL.** Two real pages (`/fr/` and `/nl/`), not a JavaScript toggle, so each Ads campaign can point at its own URL and the message match is exact. Put the switch
in the header as "FR | NL", and set hreflang on both. Auto detection by browser language is a trap for Brussels, where many people browse in a language they do not want to be sold in.
- **Prices, honestly.** Three rules. Use "à partir de" / "vanaf" with a real floor price that Roro will actually honour. State VAT treatment (particulier: TVA comprise, and note the 6
percent rate case for homes over 10 years old if Roro confirms he applies it). State the déplacement rule in the same block, either "déplacement inclus" or the exact amount. Then the
promise line: "Le prix vous est annoncé avant de commencer" / "De prijs wordt gezegd voor we beginnen". Never publish a teaser like the 20 euro competitor.
- **WhatsApp.** Secondary, smaller, and labelled for what it is: "Envoyer une photo par WhatsApp" / "Stuur een foto via WhatsApp". A photo of the blockage genuinely helps the phone
quote, so this button earns its place instead of splitting the CTA.

### 1.8 Legal musts, Belgium

Required on every business website under article III.74 of the Code de droit économique, as listed by FOD Economie: name, address of the seat, contact details including an e-mail
address, the enterprise number (BCE/KBO), the VAT number written as BTW BE / TVA BE plus the number, the supervisory authority where the activity is regulated, and a code of conduct
where one applies.
https://news.economie.fgov.be/203683-deze-info-moet-u-zeker-vermelden-op-uw-bedrijfswebsite/
https://www.securex.be/nl/zelfstandig-zijn/rechtsbijstand-ondernemers/wettelijke-verplichtingen-website

Prices shown to consumers must be the total price the consumer pays, VAT included.

Cookie banner, per the Belgian APD/GBA checklist: a "Refuser tout" button as visible and as easy to reach as "Accepter tout", no pre ticked boxes, scrolling or continued browsing is not
consent, and cookie walls are not allowed. Nothing that is not strictly necessary may load before consent, and that includes the Google Ads tag.
https://www.autoriteprotectiondonnees.be/professionnel/actualites/2023/10/20/lapd-publie-une-checklist-pour-une-utilisation-correcte-des-cookies
https://www.cookiebot.com/fr/apd-belgique-cookies/

Also needed: a privacy policy page (what is collected, why, how long, rights, contact) and a cookie policy page. Both in FR and NL.

### 1.10 How we measure

Four events, all of them worth money:
1. **call_click** on any tel: link (sticky bar, hero, service cards, footer). Primary conversion.
2. **calls from ads**, reported by Google via the forwarding number (see 2.6).
3. **whatsapp_click** on any wa.me link. Secondary conversion, lower value.
4. **scroll_75** and **time_on_page_30s**, as diagnostics only, never as conversions.

Report weekly: impressions, clicks, cost, call clicks, reported calls, cost per call, and the search terms that produced them.

## PART 2: GOOGLE ADS, WEEK ONE

### 2.1 Budget reality check, read this first

Belgian CPC benchmarks, DataforSEO query on 264 commercial keywords across 8 Belgian SME sectors, location Belgium, language French, July 2026: median CPC across all sectors 1,88 euro,
**plumber median 11,94 euro with a maximum of 33,13 euro**, the most expensive of the eight sectors measured. The same study models a cost per contact of roughly 239 to 398 euro for a
plumber at a 3 to 5 percent conversion rate.
https://www.nexuro-digital.com/en/blog/the-nexuro-blog-1/combien-coute-vraiment-google-ads-pour-une-pme-belge-benchmarks-2026-par-secteur-105

Cross check, US market: average plumbing CPC 8,45 dollar, emergency terms 12 to 22 dollar and up to 25 to 45 dollar on "emergency plumber near me", with 12 to 16 percent conversion
rates because these are emergencies and nobody comparison shops with a flooded kitchen.
https://www.webtonic.io/blog/plumbing-google-ads-statistics

**Not found:** a published Keyword Planner figure for the Belgian terms "débouchage" or "ontstopping" specifically. The plumber benchmark above is the closest published proxy, and
débouchage terms should sit under the generic "plombier" terms because they are narrower. Plan on 4 to 10 euro per click and correct with real data in week one.

What that means for the budget:
- 300 euro per month is 10 euro per day, which at 5 to 8 euro per click is **1 to 2 clicks a
day**. That is not a campaign, that is a lottery ticket, and the conversion data will never arrive. If 300 euro is the ceiling, spend it in a narrow window (see 2.4) rather than
spreading it across 24 hours and 30 communes.
- 1.000 euro per month is 33 euro per day, roughly 4 to 8 clicks a day, 120 to 240 clicks a
month. At a 10 percent call rate that is 12 to 24 calls a month, at 40 to 85 euro per call. That is a real test.
- **Recommendation to Roro: 750 to 1.000 euro per month for the first two months**, then decide
on real numbers. Say the arithmetic out loud rather than promising results.

### 2.2 Campaign structure, week one

Search only. Two campaigns, split by language.

- **Campaign 1: FR, Search, Brussels plus Vlaams-Brabant.** Language setting French.
- **Campaign 2: NL, Search, Vlaams-Brabant plus Brussels.** Language setting Dutch.

**Why separate campaigns and not separate ad groups.** Budget control is the reason. Language in Google Ads targets the interface language, not the query language, so the two languages
will compete for the same auctions if they sit in one budget, and the cheaper language eats the budget. Separate campaigns let us give FR 60 percent and NL 40 percent (Brussels is
largely French speaking, Vilvoorde and the rest of Vlaams-Brabant is Dutch speaking), see the true cost per call per language, and pause one without touching the other. It also lets
each campaign point at its own language URL, which is what makes the message match work.

### 2.6 Conversion tracking

**Calls from ads: yes, Google forwarding numbers work in Belgium.** Google lists Belgium among the countries where forwarding numbers are available, with both local and toll free
numbers. https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2382961?hl=en So enable call reporting, use a Google forwarding number in the call asset, and count a call as a conversion at a
duration threshold. **Set the threshold at 45 seconds** for week one (long enough to skip misdials and wrong numbers, short enough not to lose a real "can you come to Grimbergen now"
call), and review it against Roro's own account of which calls were real.

**Website call clicks:** a Google Ads conversion action of type "Clicks to your phone number on your website", fired by a GTM click trigger on `Click URL contains tel:`. Count "one per
click" for diagnostics, then move to "one" per session once volume allows.

**WhatsApp clicks:** GTM click trigger, type Just Links, condition `Click URL contains wa.me` or `contains web.whatsapp.com`, firing a Google Ads conversion tag and a GA4 event
`whatsapp_click`. It fires before the redirect, so it works on mobile. It only proves the click, never that a message was sent, so give it a lower conversion value than a call.
https://boei.help/blog/track-whatsapp-clicks-google-tag-manager/

**Proving the tag on the wire before launch.** A pre launch test conversion can never show up in the Ads interface, so do not wait for it. On the live domain, after accepting cookies,
run in the browser console: `performance.getEntriesByType('resource').map(r => r.name).filter(n => /googleads|doubleclick|google-analytics|googletagmanager/.test(n))` and confirm the
gtag/js load and the conversion beacon appear after a stubbed tel: click. Then treat the first real post launch conversion as the second proof. This is exactly the method used on the
taxi side and it has held up twice.

**Also check consent mode.** Because the APD requires a real reject option, the tag must run in Consent Mode v2 with `ad_storage` denied until consent. Verify that a rejected visitor
fires no conversion beacon at all, and that an accepting visitor does.

**If Google forwarding numbers ever fall short** (for example if Roro wants call recording, or a per keyword attribution of offline outcomes), the call tracking tools that operate in
Belgium include CallRail, Nimbata and Delacon, all of which provide Belgian numbers and integrate with Google Ads. For week one they are an unnecessary cost.

### 2.7 Bidding

New account, no conversion history. Sequence:

1. **Week 1 and 2: Maximize clicks with a maximum CPC cap.** Set the cap at 6 euro FR and 5 euro NL, which sits below the 11,94 euro plumber median so we buy the cheaper, more specific
débouchage auctions rather than the generic plombier ones. Manual CPC is the alternative and is equally defensible, but maximize clicks with a cap gets the data faster with less
babysitting.
2. **From roughly 15 to 30 conversions in a 30 day window: Maximize conversions.** The commonly cited threshold is at least 15 to 30 conversions per month before smart bidding has
enough signal, and 30 is the safer number. https://pillarsmedia.my/blog/when-to-switch-from-maximize-clicks-to-maximize-conversions/
3. **Only after that: Target CPA**, set at roughly 20 percent above the observed cost per call. Not before, or the campaign will simply stop serving.

Do not let Google's recommendations page talk us into Maximize conversions on day one with no conversion data and a 10 to 33 euro per day budget.

### 2.8 Local Services Ads: verdict for Belgium

**Available in principle, worth an eligibility check, but not the week one plan.** Belgium is on Google's list of Local Services Ads countries, and "Plumbers" is one of the 30 eligible
service categories listed on the Belgian help page, with the Google Verified badge rather than the US "Google Guaranteed" wording.
https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841?hl=en&co=GENIE.CountryCode%3DBE Whether the category is actually live at Vilvoorde postcode level is only answerable through
Google's own eligibility check, which needs the business details, so that is a Fady task in week two, not a claim to make now.

Two cautions. First, LSA requires business verification, insurance proof and background or licence checks, which takes time we do not have in week one. Second, Google is migrating Local
Services Ads into Performance Max campaigns with pay per lead goals: US home service providers including plumbing start moving in August 2026, and **non US accounts move in 2027**. New
local campaigns must still be created at g.co/localservices for now.
https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17213585
So: run Search now, put the LSA eligibility check and the verification paperwork on the week two list, and expect the pay per lead model to arrive through Performance Max later.

### 2.9 Performance Max: verdict for week one

**No.** Three reasons. It needs roughly 30 conversions a month before the algorithm has signal, which we will not have. It cannot be steered by keyword, so an emergency drain budget
leaks into display and video placements. And the documented pattern for small service accounts is that Search first, PMax later, produces a lower cost per lead: one worked example moved
from 45 pounds per lead on PMax alone to 28 pounds after building the Search campaign first. https://aimpro.co.uk/performance-max-campaigns-small-business/ Revisit PMax at month three,
only if Search is consistently profitable and the budget grows.

### 2.10 The first two weeks, daily routine

**Every day, 10 minutes:** 1. Did the ads serve, and did the budget run out before the evening? (If yes, the schedule or the geo is too wide for the money.) 2. Search terms report, last
24 hours. Add negatives. This is the whole job in week one. 3. Any call clicks and reported calls? Match them against what Roro says his phone did. 4. Is the landing page up, and does
the sticky call button still work on a real phone?

**Twice in week one and once in week two:**
- Check the ad schedule report by hour, and the location report by commune.
- Check device split and confirm mobile is where the money went.
- Check that no ad is disapproved and that the call asset is serving.

**What to leave alone in the first 14 days:** bids per keyword, the bidding strategy, the landing page headline, and the ad copy. Nothing has statistical meaning yet, and changing two
things at once destroys the read. The only daily edits are negatives and pausing an obviously broken keyword (high spend, zero clicks to call, irrelevant search terms).

**First report to Roro, one page, every Monday:**
- Spent this week, in euro.
- Clicks, and average cost per click.
- Call clicks on the site, plus calls reported by Google, plus WhatsApp clicks.
- Cost per call.
- The five search terms that cost the most, and what we did about them.
- One sentence: what we change next week, and what we leave alone.
- One question for him: which of the calls turned into paid jobs. Without that answer the whole
exercise is measuring noise, so ask it every single week.
