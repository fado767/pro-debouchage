### 1.8 Legal and technical

- **No enterprise number (BCE/KBO) anywhere on the site.** Belgian law requires the enterprise number and identification data to be easily, directly and permanently accessible on a professional website, for every self-employed person, company or non-profit, including a brochure site with no online sales. See https://news.economie.fgov.be/203681-informations-obligatoires-sur-le-site-web-de-votre-entreprise/ and https://www.plushaut.be/mentions-legales-requises/ (article XII.6 of the Code de droit économique, https://www.ipi.be/kb/la-profession/mentions-obligatoires/mentions-obligatoires-sur-votre-site-internet-et-reseaux-sociaux). Today the site is not compliant.
- Cookie banner: served by cookieadmin.net. Categories Necessary (always active), Functional, Analytical, Advertisement, Unclassified. Buttons: Reject All, Save My Preferences, Accept All. The buttons and category names are in English on a French-only site.
- Footer: "© 2025 Pro Debouchage | Créé par : printpress.be". The year is stale (2025, and it is 2026).
- Images seen: logo-site.png, home-header1.jpg, home-1.jpeg, 01.png (the same icon reused six times for six different services), 222.jpg (file alt "Plumber man"), Logo-PNG-Stroke.png. Filenames like "222.jpg" and "01.png" are stock theme assets, not the client's own photos.
- Gallery: roughly 28 images of real work, two of them labelled "Curage, canalisation" and "Vidange de fosse septique". No before and after pairs, no van, no team.

### 1.9 What is GOOD and worth keeping

- **The service list is real and complete.** Fourteen services covering the whole job, from a blocked sink to septic emptying to camera inspection with an insurance report. That is a genuine asset and most competitors list less.
- **The gallery has about 28 photos of actual jobs.** Real proof material we can crop, caption and reuse. It beats stock photography.
- **24/7, 365 is stated plainly** and is the right promise for this trade.
- "Rapport détaillé pour assurance" is a genuine differentiator and is buried. Most competitors do not mention insurance reports at all.
- Professional equipment named specifically (hydrocureuse haute pression, inspection camera, FlexMatic+ and the rest). Specific beats vague.
- The phone number is a real mobile and appears on every page.

### 1.10 Conversion problems, ranked

1. **French only.** Vilvoorde is a Dutch-language commune in Vlaams-Brabant. Half the searches in the home market are Dutch (ontstopping, riool ontstoppen, verstopping). The site cannot rank for or convert any of them.
2. **The fake stats, the fake team and the sample page.** They break trust the moment anyone scrolls, and they break our own rule.
3. **No prices at all.** The best competitors publish forfaits (see section 3). This trade's number one customer fear is the invoice, see section 4.
4. **No service-area list.** No commune pages, no "we come to Vilvoorde, Grimbergen, Zaventem, Machelen". Both a ranking problem and a trust problem.
5. **Cookie banner with Accept All highlighted and English labels** on a French page. Friction on first paint, on mobile, for a customer with water on the floor.
6. **No enterprise number, no mentions légales, no privacy policy.** Legally required, and in this trade specifically it is a trust signal (see section 4.4: the standard consumer advice is to check the BCE number).
7. **No reviews shown.** Competitors show 199 to 6,467 ratings. Pro Débouchage shows none.
8. **The .com domain.** Belgian trust signal is .be. Every serious local competitor found in this research uses .be.
9. **Gradient logo**, which reads as a template and does not survive a small favicon or a van sticker.
10. **No response time promise.** Competitors promise 20, 30, 45 or 60 minutes. Pro Débouchage says "rapidement".
11. Contradiction between "plus de 20 ans" and "1+ years experience" on the same screen.
12. One icon reused for all six services, stale 2025 copyright, "gallerie" misspelt, English leftovers.

### 2.2 What this means, and what is unproven

- **"Pro Débouchage" is not a registered name.** It does not appear in KBO as a name, an abbreviation or a commercial name for enterprise 1013.051.865, and a general search for a Belgian company called "Pro Débouchage" tied to pro-debouchage.com returned nothing. It is a trading name and website brand only. Under Belgian rules a commercial name in use should be registered in the KBO. Not doing so is a loose end, not a disaster, but the website must still show the legal entity and its number.
- **The registered phone (0472 93 32 22) is not the website phone (0480 649 649), and the registered email (hb.debouchage@gmail.com) is not the website email.** That is normal for a young business but it means we cannot prove from public data alone that HB Débouchage is Roro's company. The address match is exact and the trade matches exactly, so the inference is strong, but it is an inference.
- **We do not know the relation between "Roro", "Afram", and "Oualid Hamdoun".** Roro may be a nickname for Oualid, or Oualid may be a partner, or the address may be shared. This has to be asked, not assumed.
- **The 2 September 2024 start date contradicts "plus de 20 ans d'expérience" on the website.** The claim can still be honest if it means Roro's or Afram's personal years in the trade. Written as it is now, on a company page, it reads as the company's age, and the company is under two years old. We will need to reword it (for example, "vakman met meer dan 20 jaar ervaring" attached to the person, if he can back it up).

### 3.3 Synthesis

**What EVERY competitor says (table stakes, we cannot win on these, we lose without them):**
1. 24/7, 7 days a week. Sixteen out of sixteen.
2. A phone number as the primary call to action, click-to-call, repeated on every screen.
3. Fast intervention, usually with a number: 20, 30, 45 or 60 minutes.
4. Devis gratuit, no obligation.
5. The full service list: WC, évier, douche, égout, sterput, curage, camera, fosse septique.
6. Professional equipment named (hydrocureuse, camera, furet).
7. A long list of communes served.

**What only the BEST say:**
1. **Published forfait prices per job type.** Debouchage Turbo, AQD, SOS, GAM, Brupro, RIOOL Team, Ontstoppingsservice. Roughly half the field. This is the sharpest divide in the market.
2. **The price promise in words, not just a number.** L'Expert Débouchage: "le prix annoncé = le prix facturé". AQD: "prix annoncé avant intervention, sans surprise".
3. **Published night and weekend surcharges, up front.** Only L'Expert Débouchage and Ontstoppingsservice do this. Everyone else says "supplement by phone", which is exactly the ambiguity customers fear.
4. **A real, countable review score.** Debouchage 77 (199 at 4.9), Rioolprobleem Kwijt (436 at
   9.1/10). Most show nothing, or unverifiable self-declared numbers.
5. **A warranty on the work.** SD Débouchage says up to 24 months. Almost nobody else does.
6. **Bilingual.** Only IQ Plumbing and Debouchage 77 have both FR and NL. In Vilvoorde this is a glaring hole in the field.
7. **A visible VAT number.** AQD, SD, Debouchage 77, GAM, Rioolprobleem Kwijt. Roughly a third.
8. Insurance report as a named deliverable, and professional liability insurance stated (Brupro).

**FIVE GAPS WE CAN OWN**

1. **Dutch and French on one site, for Vilvoorde.** We sit in a Dutch-speaking commune where almost the whole competitive field is French-only or Dutch-only. Two languages, both first class, is the cheapest structural advantage available and nobody local has taken it.
2. **The full price grid, day and night, published.** Not "from X", but a table with the day price, the evening price, the weekend price, and what is included (travel, first hour, machine). Two competitors out of sixteen do this. It answers the number one fear directly (section 4.4).
3. **The named human who comes.** Every competitor is a faceless call centre voice. Afram, with his face, his name, and the van he arrives in, is something no franchise can copy. It is also exactly the antidote to the Halle-Vilvoorde scam network (section 4.5), which is anonymous by design.
4. **Proof, on the page.** We have about 28 real job photos already. Captioned before and after, with the commune and the job type, that is more concrete proof than anyone in this list shows.
5. **The insurance report, made into a promise.** "Photo report and camera footage for your insurer, included." It is already in their service list, nobody markets it, and it is worth real money to a customer with a flooded cellar.

### 4.3 Seasonality

Sources: https://ontstoppen-aalten.nl/seizoensgebonden-rioolproblemen-aalten-herfst-winter/ and https://ontstoppen-zeist.nl/seizoensgebonden-rioolproblemen-zeist-herfst-winter/

- September is named as the moment for preventive cleaning, before leaf fall. That is a real, dated campaign hook for curage and for the maintenance offer.
- Heavy rain drives the pompage cave inondée demand. Not seasonal in a fixed way, it is event-driven, and it is the moment to raise ad budget within hours.

### 4.4 Customer pain points and fears

1. **The invoice.** The dominant fear. A price announced on the phone that becomes something else on paper. A documented Test Achats complaint: advertised "from 59.00 EUR", invoiced 154 EUR (https://www.test-achats.be/plainte/plaintes-publiques/prix-exhorbitant/CPTBE01111690-70). Another: a technician who arrived late and waited until after hours to apply a 50% surcharge, then charged
   461.10 EUR for 20 minutes of work (https://www.test-achats.be/plainte/plaintes-publiques/arnaque-et-prix-exhorbitant/CPTBE01121243-20).
2. **Not turning up, or turning up hours late.** Made worse when lateness is used deliberately to trigger the night rate, as in the complaint above.
3. **Damage.** Breaking the pipe, the tiles or the wall. Brupro markets an explicit no-break method and professional liability insurance, which tells us the fear is real enough to sell against.
4. **Not coming at night, or the night price being invented on the spot.**
5. **Cowboys and arnaque.** See 4.5. This is not a vague worry in this specific area, it is a prosecuted pattern.
6. **The job not actually being fixed.** Advice to consumers explicitly includes testing the drain after the technician leaves, because a partial clear means a second paid visit later. Source: https://de-coster.be/page/debouchage-canalisations-bruxelles-tubize-arnaques.html . The same page lists the other red flags: a quoted amount that looks far too high (it cites 2,000 EUR excl VAT for a standard job as absurd), and a technician who turns up with a pressure washer and an unmarked car instead of proper equipment and a branded van.

### 4.5 The scam problem, and why it is our biggest opportunity

A 2025 investigation documented a plumber scam network with **over 265 victims between 2020 and 2025
in the Halle-Vilvoorde judicial district**, which is exactly Pro Débouchage's home market. Tactics:
manufacture urgency, exaggerate the problem, invent repairs, intimidate customers who hesitate.
Invoices cited include 10,000 EUR to unblock a toilet, 900 EUR for a tap, 1,300 EUR for a float,
sometimes ten times the phone quote. The article gives honest reference points: 65 to 75 EUR/h with
travel included for plumbing, and around 165 EUR/h maximum for drain clearing.
Source: https://www.moustique.be/notre-epoque/nos-enquetes/2025/10/17/comment-ne-plus-tomber-plus-dans-le-piege-des-plombiers-arnaqueurs-on-a-mene-lenquete-sur-un-reseau-qui-fait-des-centaines-de-victimes-chaque-annee-Q3DESU3DSZFZRJE43W37BN3X2M/

The SPF Economie received over 1,000 reports of this kind in 2024, up 27% in one year. Test Achats
says it is overwhelmed with complaints and has formally appealed to the SPF Economie.
Source: https://www.moustique.be/notre-epoque/les-infos/2025/10/19/pourquoi-est-ce-que-vous-vous-faites-encore-arnaquer-par-votre-plombier-malgre-toutes-les-mise-en-garde-XZMJLF6KNNGVTBAIHNRKDOZ7AI/

Other named practices: invoicing 21% VAT where 6% applies, and sending two workers to a simple
unblocking to double the labour line (same source).

**What consumers are told to check**, and therefore what our page must show without being asked:
verify the company in the BCE, check the VAT number on VIES, insist on a written estimate, confirm
the standard hourly rate, and find a trusted tradesman BEFORE the emergency.

That advice is a specification for our landing page. Enterprise number visible, prices published,
written quote promised, hourly rate stated, a named face. Every one of those is a competitor gap.

**Note for the ads brief:** one of these articles argues that a good professional does not need to
pay Google to appear first. That is consumer-press opinion, not fact, but it tells us the sentiment
exists and that our ad landing page has to look like a real local business, not a lead-gen funnel.

### 4.6 The emergency customer journey

There is no published Belgian study in these sources measuring search-to-call time, so treat the
following as inference from what the market itself promises, not as measured data.

1. Something overflows. The customer reaches for a phone, not a laptop. Mobile first, always.
2. They search a short urgent phrase and they call from the results, often without reading a full page. This is why every competitor puts a tel: link above the fold and why several buy the call-now ad format.
3. They call two or three numbers. Whoever answers, and answers with a price, wins. AQD advertises picking up in "less than 2 minutes" (https://aqd-debouchage.be/debouchage-urgent/), and describes the call itself as: state the location, the type of problem, the signs observed.
4. The promise that closes the call is a time and a price. 20 minutes (AQD), 30 minutes (SD, Debouchage 77, https://debouchagegabriel.be/), 45 minutes (GAM), 1 hour (SOS, Plombier Bruxelles). A customer review on a competitor page reports calling at 18:45 on a Friday and the technician arriving at 19:45 (https://www.iq-plumbing.be/nl/ontstoppingsdienst-vilvoorde), which is what "fast" actually looks like in practice.
5. Total elapsed time from search to booked call is best measured in minutes, not hours. Practical consequence: **an unanswered phone is a lost job, and a page that takes three seconds to paint or throws a cookie wall first is a lost job.**

## 5. GOOGLE BUSINESS PROFILE: THE ADDRESS PROBLEM

Primary source throughout: Google's "Guidelines for representing your business on Google",
https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177

### 5.1 (a) P.O. boxes and virtual offices, quoted

- "P.O. boxes or mailboxes located at remote locations aren't acceptable."
- "If your business rents a physical mailing address but doesn't operate out of that location, also known as a virtual office, that location isn't eligible for a Business Profile."
- "Service-area businesses can't list a 'virtual' office unless that office is staffed during business hours."
- On co-working: "Businesses can't list an office at a co-working space unless that office maintains clear signage, receives customers at the location during business hours, and is staffed during business hours by your business staff."
- The mailbox line that people misread as permission: "If you must specify a mailbox or suite number: Enter your physical address as 'Address Line 1.' Enter your mailbox or suite number as 'Address Line 2.'" That is about a suite number at an address you actually occupy. It is not permission to use a mail-drop.

### 5.2 (b) Service-area business with a hidden address

- Google: "If you're a service-area business, you should hide your business address from customers."
- The address is still ENTERED and still verified. Hiding controls what the public sees, not what Google checks. Hiding a non-compliant address does not make it compliant: "Hiding the address doesn't circumvent the policy violation." Source: https://www.sterlingsky.ca/google-business-profile-virtual-office-guidelines/
- The staffing requirement is the crux. An office only qualifies if it is staffed during business hours **by your own staff**, not by the mail company's staff (same source). A mailbox at Guldenschaapstraat 6 fails this test by definition.
- Google's own recommended pattern for a service-area business: use a home address, hide it, and set the service area (same source).

### 5.3 (c) Video verification, what Google wants to see

Compiled from https://searchengineland.com/google-get-service-area-business-verified-461330 ,
https://www.localfalcon.com/blog/the-ultimate-guide-to-google-business-profile-video-verification and
https://support.google.com/business/community-guide/342311022/how-to-verify-your-google-business-profile-everything-you-need-to-know-about-google-verification

One continuous unedited video, at least 30 seconds, steady and well lit, showing in one take:
1. **Location proof.** Recognisable street signs or landmarks inside the claimed service area, matching what the profile says.
2. **The vehicle.** The van, branded with the business name if possible, licence plate visible.
3. **Tools and equipment.** The hydrocureuse, the camera set, the furets, in the van or at the premises. For a drain business this is the strongest single element.
4. **Proof you manage the business.** Operating the equipment, opening the locked van, accessing tools only staff would have.
5. **Documents.** An invoice, a permit, or a utility bill carrying the business name exactly as it appears on the profile.

For Pro Débouchage this is genuinely favourable. A branded van full of specialised drain equipment,
filmed on a street in Vilvoorde, is close to an ideal video verification. Points 2, 3 and 4 are the
easiest parts of the whole exercise for this trade.

**One trap:** the documents in point 5 must carry the business name as the profile shows it. If the
profile says "Pro Débouchage" and every invoice says "HB Débouchage BV", that is a mismatch. See
section 7 FLAGGED.

### 5.4 (d) Does a mailbox address block verification, or get you suspended later?

Both, at different times.
- It often simply fails verification. Postcards to virtual offices go undelivered or unclaimed.
- If it does get verified and Google finds out later, the outcome reported is a **hard suspension**, which removes the profile entirely rather than letting you correct it. Recovering from a hard suspension is far harder than getting it right the first time. Source: https://www.sterlingsky.ca/google-business-profile-virtual-office-guidelines/
- Independent guides list virtual office and P.O. Box addresses among the standard 2026 suspension triggers, alongside keyword-stuffed names and rapid address edits. Sources: https://www.dmnetsolutions.com/google-business-profile-rules/ , https://rankai.ai/articles/service-area-business-google-business-profile-guide
- Timing matters commercially. A suspension usually lands after the profile has been earning reviews and calls, so the loss is not "we start late", it is "we lose everything we built".

### 5.5 RECOMMENDATION

**Do not use Guldenschaapstraat 6 as the Google Business Profile address if Roro's description of it
as a mail-drop is accurate.** It is the exact fact pattern Google's policy names, it risks failing
verification, and it risks a hard suspension later once the profile is valuable.

What to do instead, in order of preference:

1. **Best: the address where the van is kept overnight.** Roro's or Afram's home, or a garage, yard, lock-up or depot inside the service area. Set the profile up as a service-area business, enter that address, **hide it**, and define the service area as Vilvoorde plus the surrounding communes. This is Google's own recommended pattern, it survives video verification (the van and the equipment are physically there), and the public never sees the private address.
2. **Acceptable if a real premises exists.** If there is an actual workshop or depot where a person is present during stated hours, it can be a hybrid listing with a visible address. Only if it is genuinely staffed.
3. **Never: the mail-drop**, whether shown or hidden. Hiding it does not fix it.

Two things stay unchanged regardless of the GBP choice: the **legal** address on invoices and in the
website footer remains the registered seat (Guldenschaapstraat 6, plus the enterprise number),
because that is the KBO entry. Google's address rule and the legal-seat rule are separate questions
and they are allowed to have different answers.

**Ask Roro before we touch the profile.** The whole decision hangs on one question: where does the
van sleep, and is that address inside the service area.
